How to play AIrks

Your faction is racing for control of the Hollow Verge, six star clusters on the edge of known space. Power is your score. A game runs for up to 5 chapters (3 in a Short game, 2 in a Quick game, which is what Play now starts). It stops after any chapter that ends with someone on 33 Power or more with 2 players, 30 with 3, or 27 with 4, or after the final chapter. The most Power wins.

Chapters and rounds

  • At the start of a chapter each player draws 6 action cards. A card shows a suit, a number and some action pips. Two or three players use the 2 to 6 of every suit; four players add the 1s and 7s.
  • Whoever holds the initiative opens the round with a lead card and gets one action for each pip. Then each other player answers with one card: Outplay (same suit, higher number: you get all its pips), Switch (a different suit: 1 action from your own card) or Echo (any card, face down: 1 action from the lead card's suit).
  • The highest Outplay wins the initiative for the next round. Or grab it outright by adding a second card face down (with 4 players, Outplaying with a 7 also grabs it).
  • The initiative holder may pass rather than lead. A chapter is over when hands run out or every player still holding cards passes in turn.

Suits and actions

  • Civic: Tax, Repair, Lobby. Strike: Battle, Move, Claim. Forge: Build, Repair. Transit: Move, Lobby.
  • Tax one of your cities, or a rival's city in a system you control (and take one of their agents hostage). You gain that planet's resource.
  • Build a city or shipyard in a free slot where you already have a piece, or launch a ship from your shipyard (one per shipyard each turn). If a rival controls the system, what you build starts damaged.
  • Move any number of your ships into a neighbouring system. Ships setting out from your shipyard may Gate-jump: they keep travelling from gate to gate until they reach a planet or a gate a rival controls.
  • Repair one damaged ship or building anywhere. Lobby: place an agent on a Council card. Claim: take a Council card on which you have the most agents; any rival agents on it become your hostages.
  • You control a system when you have more undamaged ships there than any single rival.

Ambitions

Only the player who leads can declare an ambition, and the lead card's number picks which one (2 Prosperity, 3 Dominion, 4 Conquest, 5 Heritage, 6 Insight; a 7 picks any, a 1 picks none). Declaring takes the best free ambition marker and drops the lead card's number to 0, which makes it easy to Outplay. When the chapter ends, each declared ambition pays out: the leader takes the larger number on its markers, the runner-up the smaller one. If players tie for the lead they each take the smaller number. You score nothing for an ambition unless you have at least one of what it counts.

  • 💰 Prosperity: Alloy and Plasma. ⛓️ Dominion: hostages. ⚔️ Conquest: spoils. 🏺 Heritage: Artifacts. 🔮 Insight: Crystals. Ally cards count toward their resource type (Arms allies count toward none).
  • The markers begin at 5/3, 3/2 and 2/0. After every chapter the lowest marker that has not been flipped turns over to its larger side (4/2, then 6/3, then 9/4). Spoils and hostages go back to their owners when Conquest or Dominion pays out.
  • Your player board: a 2nd city opens a resource slot, a 3rd gives +2 Power on every ambition you win outright, a 4th opens two more slots and a 5th opens one more and lifts that bonus to +5.

Boosts (spend resources before you use any pips)

  • ⬢ Alloy: one Build or Repair. ⛽ Plasma: one Move. 🚀 Arms: your card's pips can Battle this turn too. 🏺 Artifact: one Claim. 🔮 Crystal: one action from the lead card's suit.

Battle

  • Choose a system where you have ships and a rival is present. Roll one die per ship (at most 6 of any kind): Light (safe, hits half the time), Heavy (more hits, but some strike your own ships and the defender can intercept) or Plunder (only against buildings, or a rival with none there: hits buildings and rolls keys, but can backfire).
  • Results apply in this order: hits on your own ships; then, if anyone rolled an intercept, one hit on you for each undamaged defending ship; then hits on their ships (carrying over to buildings once their ships are gone); then building hits; then keys, spent to steal resources and ally cards (each slot and card shows its key cost).
  • One hit damages an undamaged piece or destroys a damaged one. Rival pieces you destroy become your spoils. Destroying a city stirs Unrest: you lose that resource type and its ally cards and cannot spend it any more. It also lets you ransack the Council: take a card holding the defender's agents, and every rival agent on it becomes your spoils.
  • AIrks assigns hits for you: hits on your own ships damage undamaged ships first, hits on rival ships finish off damaged ships first, and building hits strike shipyards before cities.

Two players

The resources of the two unused clusters sit on the ambition boxes as a phantom third player. Once per chapter, the player without the initiative may redraw their hand.

Controls

Tap a card in your hand, then pick how to play it. On your turn, tap an action button, then a highlighted system on the map or a highlighted Council card. Moves and battles open a small picker for ship counts or dice.
